Communication Standards

Understanding the common technical rules and specifications that allow networking devices and technologies from different manufacturers to work together.

What Is a Communication Standard?

A communication standard is a set of agreed technical specifications and requirements that define how a particular technology or system should operate.

Standards help different devices, manufacturers, software systems, and networks work together in a predictable and compatible way.

For example, imagine buying a Wi-Fi router from one company and a laptop from another company. You expect the laptop to connect to the router without both devices being made by the same manufacturer.

This is possible because networking technologies follow common standards that define how devices should communicate.

Why Do We Need Communication Standards?

Networks contain many different devices. These devices may be produced by different companies and may use different operating systems and hardware.

Without common standards, every manufacturer could design its own completely different way of communicating.

A device from one manufacturer might then be unable to communicate with a device from another manufacturer.

Communication standards help prevent this problem by providing common technical specifications that manufacturers and developers can follow.

Communication Protocol vs Communication Standard

The words protocol and standard are related, but they do not mean exactly the same thing.

A protocol is a defined set of rules that controls how communication takes place.

A standard is a commonly agreed specification or set of requirements that allows technologies, devices, or systems to operate consistently and work together.

A standard may define or incorporate rules that are implemented by protocols and technologies.

Protocol

Defines rules for communication.

It can specify things such as message format, addressing, timing, error handling, or delivery procedures.

Standard

Defines agreed technical requirements or specifications.

It helps different implementations remain compatible.

A Simple Example

Imagine that several companies want to manufacture devices that can connect to the same type of network.

If there were no common specifications, each company could design the connection differently.

One company might use one type of connector, another could use a different signal method, and another could use completely different communication rules.

Devices from those companies might not work together.

A standard provides common technical requirements that manufacturers can use when designing compatible products.

This is one reason standards are extremely important in networking.

Who Creates Networking Standards?

Networking standards are developed and maintained by standards organizations and industry groups.

These organizations bring together engineers, researchers, manufacturers, and other technical experts to develop specifications for networking technologies.

Some important organizations involved in networking standards include:

  • IEEE — develops many standards used in networking, including Ethernet and Wi-Fi technologies.
  • IETF — develops and publishes Internet standards and protocols through documents known as RFCs.
  • ISO — develops international standards across many technical fields, including information technology.
  • ITU — develops international telecommunications standards and recommendations.

IEEE and Networking Standards

The Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) is an important organization in computer networking.

IEEE develops standards for many technologies used in local and wireless networks.

Two especially important examples are Ethernet and Wi-Fi.

Ethernet Standards

IEEE 802.3

IEEE 802.3 is the family of standards associated with Ethernet networks.

Ethernet is widely used for wired local area network communication.

Ethernet

Ethernet defines technologies and methods used for communication over wired network connections.

It is commonly used with network switches, routers, computers, and other network devices.

When you connect a computer to a network switch using an Ethernet cable, Ethernet standards help define how communication over that wired connection works.

Wi-Fi Standards

IEEE 802.11

IEEE 802.11 is the family of standards associated with wireless local area networking, commonly known as Wi-Fi.

Wireless Communication

Wi-Fi standards define technical characteristics that allow compatible wireless devices to communicate over radio signals.

This is why a phone, laptop, tablet, or other wireless device can connect to a wireless router or access point even when the devices come from different manufacturers, provided they support compatible Wi-Fi standards and settings.

Internet Standards

Internet communication also depends on standardized protocols and specifications.

The Internet Engineering Task Force, commonly known as the IETF, develops and publishes many of the technical standards used by the Internet.

These standards are commonly documented through Request for Comments (RFCs).

RFCs describe technical specifications, protocols, procedures, and other information related to Internet technologies.

Examples of Networking Technologies and Standards

Ethernet

Commonly associated with IEEE 802.3 and widely used for wired local networks.

Wi-Fi

Commonly associated with the IEEE 802.11 family and used for wireless local networks.

IP

Provides logical addressing and routing functions that are fundamental to Internet communication.

TCP

Provides transport services such as reliable, ordered delivery when an application uses TCP.

HTTP

Defines communication between web clients and web servers.

DNS

Provides a system for resolving domain names into information such as IP addresses.

Why Are There Different Standards?

Networking is not one single technology.

Different standards are needed because different parts of communication have different requirements.

Wired networking needs standards for things such as cables, signals, connectors, and communication methods.

Wireless networking needs standards for radio communication, frequencies, channels, and other wireless characteristics.

Internet communication needs standards for addressing, routing, application communication, and transport.

Therefore, many standards and protocols can work together during one communication session.

Interoperability

Interoperability means that different systems or technologies can work together and exchange information.

This is one of the major benefits of communication standards.

For example, your laptop may use one operating system, your router may be manufactured by another company, and the network switch may come from another manufacturer.

They can still communicate because they implement compatible networking technologies and standards.

Standards and Different Manufacturers

Standards do not mean that every device must be identical.

Manufacturers can create products with different designs, features, prices, and performance levels.

What matters is that the products follow the relevant standards well enough to communicate with compatible devices.

This allows competition and innovation while still maintaining interoperability.

Protocol vs Standard: Remember This

A protocol defines rules for communication.

A standard provides commonly agreed technical specifications or requirements that help technologies and systems work consistently and together.

Protocols and standards are closely related, but they are not interchangeable terms.

For example, Ethernet and Wi-Fi are technologies associated with IEEE standards, while protocols such as IP, TCP, HTTP, and DNS define particular communication functions.

Module Summary

Communication standards provide common technical specifications that help different networking devices and systems communicate and work together.

Standards are important because networks contain equipment from many different manufacturers and technologies.

Organizations such as IEEE, IETF, ISO, and ITU are involved in developing or maintaining standards and specifications used in networking and telecommunications.

IEEE 802.3 is associated with Ethernet, while IEEE 802.11 is associated with wireless LAN technologies commonly known as Wi-Fi.

Internet technologies also depend on standardized protocols and specifications, many of which are developed and documented through the IETF and its RFC system.

Most importantly, standards and protocols allow different parts of a network to work together. One communication can therefore involve several protocols and technologies, with each one performing a different function.
