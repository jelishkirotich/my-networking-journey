MODULE 4

Internet Connectivity

Understanding how a home network connects to the Internet, the role of ISPs, modems, routers, and different Internet connection technologies.

Introduction

A home network allows devices inside a house to communicate with each other, but most homes also need a connection to the Internet.

The Internet connection is normally provided by an Internet Service Provider, commonly called an ISP. The ISP connects the home network to the larger Internet.

In this topic, we will look at ISPs, modems, routers, DSL, cable Internet, fiber connections, coaxial splitters, and the difference between a private home network and the public Internet.

1. What Is an ISP?

ISP stands for Internet Service Provider. It is a company or organization that provides customers with access to the Internet.

When you subscribe to an Internet service, the ISP provides the connection between your home or business and its network. From there, your traffic can reach other networks on the Internet.

Simple Example

Think of an ISP as the company that provides the road connecting your home network to the rest of the Internet.

2. How Does a Home Connect to the Internet?

A typical home Internet connection can be understood as a series of connected devices.

A simple connection can be represented as:

ISP → Internet Connection → Modem or ONT → Router → Home Devices

The exact equipment depends on the type of Internet service being used. Some modern connections combine several of these functions into one device.

3. What Is a Modem?

A modem is a device that helps connect a customer's network to certain types of Internet service provided by an ISP.

The word modem comes from modulator-demodulator. Traditional modems convert signals between forms suitable for the service connection and digital network communication.

The exact function of a modem depends on the type of connection being used.

Do Not Confuse a Modem and a Router

A modem connects to the ISP's service, while a router connects devices together and manages traffic between the home network and other networks.

In many homes, one device supplied by the ISP may combine modem, router, switch, and wireless access point functions.

4. DSL Internet

DSL stands for Digital Subscriber Line. DSL technology can provide Internet access over telephone lines.

A DSL connection normally uses a DSL modem to communicate with the ISP.

Important Idea

DSL uses existing telephone-line infrastructure, but it allows data communication without being limited to traditional voice telephone communication.

5. Cable Internet

Cable Internet uses the cable infrastructure traditionally associated with cable television.

The connection commonly uses coaxial cable to carry the service toward the customer's home.

Coaxial cable can be used to carry cable television and cable Internet signals.

6. Coaxial Splitter

In some homes, a coaxial connection may be used for more than one service. A coaxial splitter can divide the incoming signal into multiple outputs.

A splitter can allow one incoming coaxial connection to feed multiple destinations.

For example, one output may be connected to a television service while another is connected to a cable modem.

Remember

A splitter does not act as a router. Its purpose is to divide the coaxial signal between connections.

7. Fiber Internet

Fiber Internet uses fiber-optic cables to carry data using light signals.

Fiber-optic connections use light to transmit data.

Fiber connections can provide high-speed Internet access and are commonly used in modern broadband networks.

ONT

In a fiber-to-the-home connection, an Optical Network Terminal (ONT) may be used to convert the optical connection into a form that the customer's network equipment can use.

Depending on the ISP and equipment, the ONT may be a separate device or may be built into another piece of network equipment.

8. The Router's Role

After the Internet connection reaches the home, the router helps distribute the connection to devices on the local network.

A home router can provide wired Ethernet connections and wireless Wi-Fi connectivity.

A router can connect the private home network to the ISP network and forward traffic between different networks.

9. Public and Private Networks

It is important to understand the difference between a private local network and the public Internet.

Private Network

A private network is a network used within a limited organization or location, such as a home, school, or office.

Devices connected to your home router normally communicate within your local network using private IP addresses.

Public Network

The Internet is a large collection of interconnected networks that allows devices and services around the world to communicate.

Private Network Public Internet
Used within a home, school, or organization Connects networks around the world
Uses local network addressing Provides access to external networks and services
Managed by the local network owner Made up of many interconnected networks

10. A Simple Home Internet Example

Imagine that you want to open a website using your laptop at home.

Step 1: Laptop

Your laptop connects to the home router through Wi-Fi or an Ethernet cable.

Step 2: Router

The router receives the request from your laptop and determines where the traffic needs to go.

Step 3: Modem or ONT

The traffic passes through the equipment responsible for connecting your home network to the ISP service.

Step 4: ISP

The ISP carries the traffic from your home connection into its network and toward the destination.

Step 5: Internet

The traffic travels through multiple interconnected networks until it reaches the server hosting the website.

Step 6: Response

The response travels back through the network to your laptop, allowing the website to appear in your browser.

11. Putting Everything Together

A basic home Internet setup can therefore be thought of as several parts working together:

  • ISP: Provides Internet access.
  • Internet connection: Carries the service toward the home.
  • Modem or ONT: Connects the home equipment to the type of service provided.
  • Router: Connects the home network to other networks and forwards traffic.
  • Switch: Provides additional wired network connections when needed.
  • Wireless access point: Provides Wi-Fi connectivity.
  • End devices: Use the network to access services and communicate.

12. What We Need to Remember

  • ISP means Internet Service Provider.
  • An ISP provides access to the Internet.
  • DSL can provide Internet service over telephone lines.
  • Cable Internet can use coaxial cable infrastructure.
  • Fiber Internet uses optical fiber and light signals.
  • A modem or ONT connects to the type of service provided by the ISP.
  • A router connects the home network to other networks.
  • A coaxial splitter divides a coaxial connection into multiple outputs.
  • A home LAN is a private local network.
  • The Internet is a collection of interconnected networks.
