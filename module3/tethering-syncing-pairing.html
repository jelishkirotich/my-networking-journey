Tethering, Syncing and Pairing

Understanding how mobile devices share internet connections, connect with other devices, and keep information up to date.

Introduction

Modern mobile devices can do more than simply connect to the internet. A smartphone can share its internet connection with another device, connect to wireless accessories, and keep information synchronized across different devices.

Three important terms used when discussing these functions are tethering, pairing, and syncing.

1. Tethering

Tethering is the process of sharing a mobile device's internet connection with another device.

For example, your phone may have mobile data but your laptop may not have an internet connection. You can use your phone to provide internet access to the laptop.

Simple Example

Your phone has mobile data → you turn on Hotspot → your laptop connects to the phone → the laptop gets internet access.

The phone acts as the device providing the internet connection. The other device uses that connection through the tethering method that has been selected.

Types of Tethering

There are three common ways a phone can share its internet connection:

Wi-Fi Hotspot

The phone creates a small Wi-Fi network that other devices can connect to.

This is useful when connecting laptops, tablets, or multiple devices.

USB Tethering

The phone is connected directly to another device using a USB cable.

The connected device can then use the phone's internet connection.

Bluetooth Tethering

The phone shares its internet connection with another device through Bluetooth.

The two devices normally need to be paired first.

Wi-Fi Hotspot

A Wi-Fi hotspot allows your phone to behave like a small wireless access point.

When the hotspot is enabled, the phone creates a Wi-Fi network with a network name called an SSID.

Another device can find that SSID in its Wi-Fi settings, enter the hotspot password, and connect to the phone.

USB Tethering

USB tethering uses a USB cable to connect the phone directly to another device.

For example, you can connect your phone to a laptop using a USB cable and enable USB tethering. The laptop can then use the phone's mobile data connection.

Example

Phone → USB cable → Laptop → Internet

Because the connection uses a physical cable, the devices do not need to communicate through a Wi-Fi hotspot.

Bluetooth Tethering

Bluetooth tethering allows a phone to share its internet connection with another device through Bluetooth.

The devices normally need to be paired first. After pairing, the second device can use the phone's network connection.

Example

Phone → Bluetooth → Laptop → Internet

2. Pairing

Pairing is the process of establishing a relationship between two devices so that they can communicate, commonly using Bluetooth.

For example, when you connect Bluetooth earbuds to your phone for the first time, the two devices usually go through a pairing process.

Common Bluetooth Pairing Examples

  • Phone + Bluetooth earbuds
  • Phone + Bluetooth speaker
  • Phone + smartwatch
  • Laptop + Bluetooth mouse
  • Laptop + Bluetooth keyboard

3. Syncing

Syncing, short for synchronization, means keeping information updated between devices or between a device and an online service.

The goal is to make sure that changes made in one place can also appear in another place.

Simple Example

You add a new contact to your phone. If your contacts are synchronized with your account, that contact may also become available on another device signed into the same account.

Examples of Syncing

Contacts

Contacts can be synchronized between a phone and an online account.

Photos

Photos can be synchronized with a cloud storage service so they can be accessed from other devices.

Calendar

Calendar events can be synchronized between a phone, computer, and online account.

Files

Files can be synchronized between a device and a cloud storage service.

Do Not Confuse These Three Terms

Term Main Purpose Example
Tethering Share an internet connection Phone shares mobile data with a laptop
Pairing Establish a connection between devices Phone paired with Bluetooth earbuds
Syncing Keep information updated Phone contacts synchronized with an account

Putting Everything Together

Imagine you are using your laptop outside and there is no available Wi-Fi network.

You can turn on your phone's mobile data and create a Wi-Fi hotspot. Your laptop then connects to the phone's hotspot.

In this example:

Tethering: Your phone shares its internet connection with the laptop.

Pairing: If you connect your phone to Bluetooth earbuds, the earbuds and phone can be paired.

Syncing: If your phone synchronizes your contacts with an online account, your contact information can stay updated across devices.

Key Idea

Tethering is about sharing internet access.

Pairing is about establishing a connection between devices.

Syncing is about keeping information updated between devices or services.
