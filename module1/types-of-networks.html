Types of Networks

Networks are described by how far they reach, how they connect and how they share resources.

1. Networks by size

The most common way to classify a network is by the area it covers. A network around one person is tiny. A network linking countries is huge. The names below are common scopes, and real networks often overlap them.

2. PAN, LAN and WLAN

TypeMeaningExample
PANPersonal Area Network. Devices around one person, usually over Bluetooth.Phone connected to earbuds and a smartwatch
LANLocal Area Network. Devices in a home, lab or building.Computers and a printer joined by a switch
WLANWireless LAN. A LAN that uses Wi-Fi through an access point.Laptops and phones on home Wi-Fi
LAN describes size and location, not the cable. A LAN can be wired, wireless or both.

Wireless gives you mobility and fewer cables. The trade offs are limited coverage, radio interference and the need to set up security properly.

3. CAN, MAN and WAN

TypeMeaningExample
CANCampus Area Network. Several LANs joined across a campus.A university linking its library, labs and hostels
MANMetropolitan Area Network. Sites linked across a city.A bank connecting its branches in one city
WANWide Area Network. Networks linked over regions, countries or continents.A company connecting offices in different countries

WANs use many technologies: fiber links, leased lines, cellular networks and satellite. A useful way to remember the scale: a LAN is one building, a MAN is a city, a WAN is the world.

4. The Internet

The Internet is the largest network of networks. It joins the networks of homes, companies, governments and service providers. It is not one giant LAN. It works because all of these networks follow the same standard protocols.

5. SAN and VPN

These two are different from the size based types above.

TypeWhat it isWhere you find it
SANStorage Area Network. A fast, specialised network that gives servers access to shared storage.Data centers and large organisations
VPNVirtual Private Network. A protected, encrypted connection across another network such as the Internet.Staff working remotely and reaching the office network
PAN, LAN, MAN and WAN describe geographic scope. A VPN describes a method of connecting, so it can run over any of them.

8. Networks working together

You never use only one type. Picture a student on campus Wi-Fi opening a website:

Phone > WLAN > campus LAN > WAN > Internet > website server

A company works the same way. Each office has wired and wireless LANs, branch offices connect over a WAN, and remote staff join through a VPN.

9. Quick check

Which network connects a phone to earbuds?

A PAN.

What does the L in WLAN stand for, and what does the W add?

Local. The W means the LAN uses wireless links.

A university links its library and labs. Which type is that?

A CAN.

Is a VPN a size category like LAN or WAN?

No. It is a protected connection method that can run over other networks.

Which design uses dedicated servers to provide services?

Client server.
