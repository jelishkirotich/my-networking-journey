1. Introduction to Network Types

A computer network is a group of connected devices that can communicate and share information or resources.

Networks can be classified in different ways. One of the most common ways is according to the geographical area they cover.

A network connecting devices around one person is much smaller than a network connecting computers across an entire country or across different continents.

Key idea: Network types help us describe the size, purpose, technology and architecture of a network.

4. Local Area Network

A Local Area Network, or LAN, connects devices within a relatively small geographical area such as a home, office, laboratory, classroom or building.

Ethernet is widely used for wired LAN connections. Wireless LANs use Wi-Fi and are commonly referred to as WLANs.

Home LAN

Connects workstations, printers, servers, switches and other business devices.

A WAN can use many different communication technologies. These may include fiber optic links, leased lines, cellular networks, satellite links and other telecommunications services.

The Internet

The Internet is the world's largest interconnected network of networks. It connects networks belonging to individuals, organizations, businesses, governments and service providers.

Important distinction: The Internet is not simply one giant LAN. It is a global system of interconnected networks that communicate using standardized protocols.

14. Network Types in the Real World

In real environments, several network types can exist together. A single organization may use LANs, WLANs, WANs, VPNs and specialized storage networks at the same time.

Example 1: University

A university may have LANs inside individual buildings. Wi-Fi provides WLAN access to students and staff. Multiple buildings can be connected through a campus network. The university may also use WAN services to connect to other campuses or external services.

Example 2: Company

A company may have wired and wireless LANs inside its offices. Branch offices in different cities can be connected using a WAN. Employees working remotely may use VPN connections to access internal resources.

Example 3: Data Center

A data center can contain server networks, storage networks, management networks and connections to external WAN infrastructure.

16. Module Summary

Networks can be classified according to their geographical scope, technology, purpose and architecture.

Key points to remember:
  • PAN connects personal devices over a very small area.
  • LAN connects devices within a relatively small local area.
  • WLAN is a wireless LAN.
  • CAN can connect multiple LANs across a campus or organization.
  • MAN can connect networks across a metropolitan area.
  • WAN connects networks across large geographical areas.
  • SAN provides specialized network access to storage resources.
  • VPN creates a protected logical connection across another network.
  • Networks can use wired or wireless communication.
  • Client-server networks use dedicated servers to provide services.
  • Peer-to-peer networks allow devices to communicate and share resources without requiring a dedicated central server.
  • Real-world networks commonly combine several network types.
